# ORA · Brand Strategy

**What this is.** The business case for ORA: positioning, market evidence, the customer, the price
logic, and the risks.

**Who it's for.** Angela first, as the document she holds the line with when month three is soft and
discounting starts to feel reasonable. Then anyone who needs the argument in numbers rather than
atmosphere: a lender, a landlord, a serious collaborator, a senior hire.

**Where it sits.** Three parts, in order. **Strategy**, why it works. **System**, what it is.
**Showcase**, see it. This is the first.

---

## Positioning

**ORA offers sixty minutes that belong to no one else.**

Every other studio in Skopje sells access: to a machine, a class, a floor. ORA offers the hour
itself. What happens inside it, what happens around it, and who she is when it ends.

Three things build the hour. Nothing enters this brand that doesn't belong to one of them.

> **The Room holds it. The Movement fills it. The Woman takes it back.**

| | Governs | Pillar |
|---|---|---|
| **The Room** | Space, material, light, hospitality | **Standard** |
| **The Movement** | The five disciplines, method, instructors | **Integration** |
| **The Woman** | Who she is, what she leaves with, community | **Return** |

The category is Macedonia's first hybrid movement studio for women. Skopje has gyms, spin studios,
and pilates studios that bolt on extras. ORA belongs to none of them. It belongs to a category that
already exists in Barcelona, London and Dubai and has simply never opened here. ORA does not enter
a competition, it opens one.

> For the woman who lives deliberately and does not compromise on standard, **ORA is Macedonia's
> first hybrid movement studio**: five disciplines, one room, one community, one hour that is hers.
> Unlike the studios she has been stitching her week together from, ORA does not ask her to choose
> between the ways her body needs to move. **A body is not built by one discipline, and neither is
> a life.**

Five disciplines are not five products. They are one practice, and her body chooses which door it
uses today. Cycling leads, because it does not exist here. Pilates converts demand that is already
proven. Strength holds the woman who trains hard already. Running is the easiest door in, because
she knows how, which makes it where the hesitant start. Yoga closes the week.

The room is the business model. 150 m² that behaves like 400: one Movement Room, one Atrium, and
the relationship between them. Other studios are transactional, sixty minutes in and out. ORA is
hospitality, and the room is built around the hour rather than the hour squeezed into the room.

Her memory is the real competition, not anything currently open in Skopje. She has trained abroad,
come home and settled. Or she never left, and watches the studios she wants on Instagram week after
week, unable to find them here. ORA is the first room in this city built to be measured against
what she has already seen, or has been watching from a distance, and to hold up.

| | Where ORA wins |
|---|---|
| **Miss Fit** | Attempts the hybrid model, cannot afford the standard. We win on the room, not the roster. |
| **SportInn** | Cycling as an identity, unisex. We give cycling as one of five ways her week moves. |
| **Reformer studios** | They serve specialisation. We serve the integrated woman: one membership, not three. |
| **Functional Planet / Fit In** | The only real fight, and only on strength. Their member is the right woman who hasn't found the right room. |

**What cannot be copied:** the founder's story, and the fact that every operational decision
descends from it. The first-mover position in a category she learns here. HUMN, the first
Macedonian women's activewear brand, co-founded by Angela, which means ORA opens to a community
that is already converted rather than one built from zero. And the Atrium as a social space, the
part competitors will build last, because they will not understand it is the reason she stays.

## Market

111 women in Skopje answered eight direct questions before a wall was painted: what they train now,
what frustrates them, whether they would try indoor cycling, what they expect to pay, what would
make them stay or leave.

62 of them use a single place or app to train. 44 use two or three, stitching a week together
across a reformer studio, a gym and an app. That second group is ORA's argument, already visible in
their behaviour before anyone made the argument to them.

Intent is proven. Asked whether she would sign up if ORA opened next month with a founding-member
offer: 36 said yes immediately, 62 said maybe if the offer was right, 10 wanted to see it first,
one said no. Angela's own bar was 100 real yes-es before committing fully. She cleared it before
the studio existed.

The complaints cluster, and the larger cluster is about conditions rather than training. Men
staring. Stuffy air. Waiting for machines. Poor ventilation. A room one woman described as
predominantly male. A second, smaller cluster runs on monotony, motivation and results that take
too long to show. Conditions are what ORA is built to fix, and conditions are where most of the
frustration sits.

Context. Average net wage in Skopje runs around €740 a month. Café culture, coffee at roughly €2,
is the closest thing the city already has to what ORA is building: a place to arrive early and stay
late. Instagram and TikTok are where she will find the room before she walks into it. Wellness as
lifestyle is still emerging here, which is the opening.

## Customer

**She has already filtered everything in her life except fitness, because nothing here has met her
standard.** Early-to-mid thirties. She owns a small business or runs a serious one. Her circle is
small and chosen. She walks Vodno, takes her coffee at Duplo or Dabov, keeps a nutritionist and a
bedtime she protects. What she eats, what she reads, who she works with: all of it already decided.
Fitness is the one filter she has not been able to apply.

She is not looking for a gym. She is looking for the room where the business does not follow her.

**What she is actually buying:** one hour with nothing required of her. No performing, no
explaining, no being looked at. Women who speak her language. A standard that finally matches the
rest of her life, in her own city.

**The woman who already recognises it:** mid-twenties to early thirties. She has never trained
abroad, or only on holiday, but she has been watching those studios on Instagram for years. She
recognises the model on sight and needs no explanation of what indoor cycling is, or why five
disciplines under one roof is different from a gym with a timetable. She is the fastest conversion
in the market, because nothing has to be taught first. She is also the reason the room travels: she
has been waiting to post this, and she already follows every account it will be measured against.
Price is more likely to be her limit than taste, which is what the intro offer and the
founding-member terms are for.

**A third worth serving:** 35 to 50, senior manager or director at a multinational or a serious
local company. Same standard, less time, more likely to buy a fixed weekly membership than to
browse a timetable.

**Who she is not:** the woman looking for cheap. Not because of her budget, but because of her eye.
She does not see the detail, and everything ORA does is detail. She will say it is expensive. She
is correct, and she is not the customer.

## Price

Price signals position. ORA sits between the €5 gym spin that reads as mass and the €14 reformer
studio that has already proven affluent women here will pay for boutique.

The architecture does two jobs at once. The drop-in is the signal and the packages are the offer. A
single class at 800 MKD sits at the top of the market and says what kind of room this is. The
packages then land where the market actually is: 76% of the women surveyed expect to pay 600 MKD or
less per class, and every ORA package of eight classes or more meets that number. The intro offer,
three classes at 500 MKD each, sits inside the largest single cluster of expectation in the survey.

That shape is right. The premium is stated once, at the door, then rewarded the moment she commits.

Single-discipline monthly memberships run 20 to 30% above local competition. That gap is the
standard, and it holds only as long as the standard is real.

When she says it is too expensive, the answer is the room. We do not lower the price to meet a
competitor. We invite her in and let the room do the work.

## Risk

**The stated risk was never demand. In Angela's own words, it is whether the instructors are
ready.** The international-standard claim gets audited in the first hour of the first class, by a
woman who already knows what that standard looks like elsewhere.

It is being managed rather than watched. Instructors train under a master coach connected to an
established hybrid studio in Dubai, online through the lead-up and in person before the doors open.
The opening moved from September to October specifically to buy that runway. If the instructors are
not ready, the date moves again before the standard does.

Four other live risks.

The room has to hold five disciplines. The workbook describes 150 m² as tight for four, and its
equipment budget covers bikes, sound, lighting and mats, with no treadmills in it. "Five
disciplines, one room" is the category claim, so the floor plan and the equipment list have to
prove it before the claim goes to print.

The credit system is unfamiliar locally. Most people here are used to a flat monthly gym fee.
Credits are a language problem with revenue consequences, and they are solved in
`02 · Brand Character` rather than with a discount.

The funnel is finite. The woman ORA is built for is a real but limited group in this city. That is
a constraint the brand accepts rather than a problem it widens its way out of.

Summer empties out. July and August are quiet for every studio in Skopje. The Atrium and the patio
are the seasonal answer, since shade and a place to sit hold a community through the months the
Movement Room cannot.

## In one paragraph

> **ORA is Macedonia's first hybrid movement studio for women: strength, running, cycling, pilates
> and yoga in one room, on one membership, in one community. It exists because a body is not built
> by a single discipline, and because the woman who has already filtered everything in her life has
> never been able to filter this. ORA gives her the hour: sixty minutes with nothing required of
> her, in a room built to the standard she keeps everywhere else, among women who speak her
> language.**
