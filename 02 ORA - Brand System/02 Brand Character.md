# ORA · Brand Character

**What this is.** How ORA speaks and behaves. Personality, tone, the words we use and refuse, how
things get named, which language goes where, how credits are explained, approved copy, and what the
team says and does at the door.

**Who it's for.** Everyone who speaks or acts as ORA. The receptionist, the instructors, whoever
runs Instagram, any copywriter or translator. If you are about to write a caption, answer a message,
or greet a woman at the door, the answer is in here.

**Where it sits.** Second of four inside System. Story is what ORA is. Character is how ORA behaves
because of it.

---

## Who ORA is

Around thirty. Speaks with intention: nothing unnecessary, every word heard. Her own style,
unmistakable. Serious most of the time, sharp when she is not. Around her you feel safe,
appreciated, free to ask anything.

> **She's not the sun. She's not the cloud. She's spring.**

---

## How ORA speaks

Short sentences, plain words. Warm, never cute. Certain, never loud. The same voice on a sign, on
Instagram and at the door.

**Say the thing, then stop.** The instinct to add one more line of reassurance is the instinct to
sound like everyone else.

**Describe the fact, not the feeling.** "The lights go down and the music comes up" does more work
than "an unforgettable experience". She can feel things without being told to.

**Address her directly.** *You* more often than *we*. ORA talks to one woman, never to an audience.

**No exclamation marks.** Certainty does not need them.

**Never sell inside the hour.** Selling happens before she arrives and at reception. Once she is in
the room, nothing is being sold to her.

**Say women, not girls, in anything written.** Spoken, in the room, warmth is the team's own.
Written, it is women.

---

## The words

**Ours.** Your hour · room · return · movement · community · rhythm · method · honest · real ·
earned · felt · built · here · now · hers · ours · healthy clubbing · coming back to yourself.

**Never ours.** "Ready to crush your goals." "Transform your body." "Snap back." "Beast mode."
"Bikini season." Emoji-stacked captions. Countdown urgency. Anything that shouts at her, and
anything that turns her body into a project she is failing.

---

## How ORA names things

Plain nouns. English for what a thing is, Macedonian for what it means.

**The ORA prefix is for permanent things only.** The ORA Index. The ORA Member Card. It is not a
decoration to attach to a promotion or a month.

**No invented compounds.** No ORAfit, no ORAlife, no ORAmove.

**Disciplines are called what they are:** strength, running, cycling, pilates, yoga. Never RIDE,
SCULPT, BURN. One-word marketing names belong to the studios ORA is defined against.

**The current roster:** the Atrium · the Movement Room · the ORA Index · the First Hour Booklet ·
the Member Card · ORA × HUMN.

## Two languages

Macedonian carries feeling. English carries function.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Never translated** | Ова е твој час. It is the one line that only works in Macedonian, and translating it destroys the double meaning. |
| **English** | Discipline names, the timetable, the app interface, wayfinding. She already recognises these words from the studios she follows. |
| **Macedonian** | The promise on the door, the welcome, the message after her first class, anything said to her rather than about the studio. |
| **Never mixed** | Not inside a single sentence. Pick the language for the sentence and finish it there. |
| **Never translated, ever** | The mark. `o / ra` is a placed file in every language. |

## Explaining credits

Most women in Skopje have only ever bought a flat monthly gym fee. Credits are unfamiliar, and
unfamiliar costs money at the point of sale. The fix is language, not a discount.

**Never lead with the word credits.** Lead with classes.

> **Wrong.** Buy a 12-credit package and redeem across disciplines.
>
> **Right.** Twelve classes. Use them on anything. Cycling one day, yoga the next.

**The sentence that does the work:**

> **One membership. Any of the five. You choose on the day.**

If she asks a second question, the answer is always the same shape: she is buying classes, not
access, and no discipline is locked behind a different payment.

## How ORA behaves

The brand is audited here more than anywhere else. This section is Angela's own protocol, held as
written.

### Before she arrives

The team already knows she is coming. The booking system gives reception every name on the day's
schedule by the hour, and the front desk reads the names at the start of the shift. They know who
has been here before. They know who is new.

**The schedule is not a list. It is fifteen women's names, each with a small story we already know.**

### The first three seconds

The first thing she notices is the room, not us. Light from the Atrium falls into the entrance. The
concrete is warm. No fluorescent strip. The air smells faintly of cedar, with no commercial cleaner
and no incense.

The second thing is the silence. Not the silence of an empty space, the silence of a considered one.
Music is playing below the threshold of being noticed.

The third thing is the woman at reception. She is not behind a counter that hides her. Not on her
phone. Not pretending to be busy. She is reading, or making a note, or arranging the tray of Member
Cards, and the moment the door opens that attention turns to whoever has walked in.

**This is the first message of the brand. The room was waiting for you. The person was here,
present, before you arrived.**

### The greeting

First time, word for word:

> *Hi. You found us. Are you Marija?*
>
> *Welcome, I'm Pavlina. Come in. Let me show you around before we start.*

### The walkthrough

Sixty seconds, ninety if she is slow. We walk her through. **We do not point.**

> *This is the Atrium. You can wait here before class, there's a smoothie bar if you want something.
> After class this is where most of the women stay for fifteen, twenty minutes. Some bring their
> laptops in the mornings. The light is good here all day.*

At the Movement Room glass:

> *That's the Movement Room. All five disciplines happen in there: strength, running, cycling,
> pilates, yoga. Each one has its own time. Today is cycling. The bikes are set up.*

At the changing rooms:

> *Changing rooms are down the hall. Lockers on the left. Showers on the right. Towels are ready for
> you.*

The walkthrough does three things at once. It gives her geographical certainty, so she never has to
ask. It shows her everything has been thought of before she needed it. And it establishes the Atrium
and the Movement Room as two different places with two different jobs, before she has used either.

### In the room

The instructor greets the women already there, asks how the last class went, and introduces the new
woman by name. During class she goes to each woman individually for position and posture. After a
well-executed series she uses the phrase *greet the woman next to you*, which is how strangers in a
dark room become a community without anyone announcing it.

**The instructor knows her name before she sits down.** Every class, every discipline.

### Leaving

Her smoothie is already being made, because she ordered before class and reception remembered.
Reception remembers what she has, and remembers when she changes her mind.

At the door, reception looks up after her:

> *Are you joining us same time tomorrow?*

## What ORA refuses

1. **Nothing we would not put in our own bodies.**
2. **No colour, material or finish that does not belong to the brand.** Not for a sponsor, not for a
   season.
3. **No one who treats this as a job.** Skill can be trained. Arrogance cannot be removed.
4. **We do not compete on price.** If price is the objection, the answer is the room.
5. **We do not become mass.** The finite funnel is a constraint we accept, not a problem we widen
   our way out of.

## Open

- **The towel line contradicts itself in the source.** Angela's workbook says towels are both
  150 MKD at reception and free of charge for training, in the same sentence. The walkthrough script
  above says only "towels are ready for you" so the team never quotes a price that may be wrong.
  Decide: free, 150 MKD, or free for class and 150 for showers.
- **Women, not girls.** A rule written here rather than found in the source. Angela says "girls"
  warmly throughout her workbook. Reverse it if the register is wrong.
