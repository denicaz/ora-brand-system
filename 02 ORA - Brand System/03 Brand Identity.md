# ORA · Brand Identity

**What this is.** How ORA looks. The mark, colour, type, art direction, material, light, sound and
scent, with the exact values a vendor needs.

**Who it's for.** Anyone making something physical or digital in ORA's name. The signage fabricator,
the printer, the web developer, the photographer, any designer who touches this after Denica. It is
built to be opened for thirty seconds at a time and closed again.

**Where it sits.** Third of four inside System. Story is what ORA is, Character is how it speaks,
this is how it looks.

# 01 · The mark

The visual system begins with the measure of an hour. The logo gives that measure a physical form: the clock, a structure designed to hold it. In the same way, ORA exists to hold an hour entirely for her — creating the space for that time to belong to her. ORA adopts this structure as its mark, turning the idea of holding time into a visual symbol.

## 1 · The four variations

The clock at four settings, each with one job.

| File | Variation | What it is | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ORA_LOGO_CLOCK` | **The Clock** | Full field | Used only as it is |
| `ORA_LOGO_FRAME` | **The Frame** | Corners, content inside | Holds what ORA believes |
| `ORA_LOGO_SIGNATURE` | **The Signature** | Reading distance | The default, everywhere |
| `ORA_LOGO_WORD` | **The Word** | No hand, paired with text | Carries the disciplines |

**The Clock.** The full field as the watch: glyphs at the corners, the hand crossing the surface.
This is the product itself, and it is treated the way the hour is sold: never cut, never subdivided,
never given content. Used only as it is, one per artifact, filling the surface alone. The cover, the
end-frame, the one poster with nothing to say.

**The Frame.** The clock opened out. The four glyphs pin the corners and the content sits inside,
which makes this the variation that carries what ORA believes: the principles, the pillars, the
reasons the studio exists. One idea per frame. A red kicker in the voice, one display word, a mono
position, mono attributes below. Never below A5, where the corners stop reading as one object.

The mono number is a clock position, not a count. `0 / 00` for the Room at the hour's start,
`0 / 30` for the Movement at its centre, `1 / 00` for the Woman at its close.

**The Signature.** `o / ra` at reading distance. The default: all general brand communication, every
partner and vendor context, every surface where ORA speaks as itself. It stands alone, or takes one
companion set small beside it, being the descriptor, the discipline stack, or a slogan. Never two on
one surface.

**The Word.** `ora` with no hand, paired with typed text: `ora ……… / strength`. For disciplines,
events and classes: anything where the subject is the content and the brand is the signature. The
pairing is set in mono, slash first, lowercase. This is why there are no sub-logos and never need to
be. `/ strength`, `/ pop-up`, `/ summer schedule` are generated by typography rather than by new
marks.

### Files

Every variation downloads in three formats. **SVG** for anything vector, **PNG** at 4× the
392 × 466 artboard for screen and quick placement, **PDF** single-page at exact artboard size for
print and vendors. All are served from `LOGO - CORRECTED`, rebuilt from vectors whose hand sits at
exactly 40°. The original `LOGO` folder is untouched, and Figma remains the master.

## 2 · Choosing the variation

| The surface is about | Use |
|---|---|
| The hour itself, whole | The Clock |
| Why ORA exists, what it holds to | The Frame |
| A discipline, a class, an event | The Word |
| The brand, speaking as itself | The Signature |

When two answers seem true at once, **the Signature wins**.

## 3 · Rules

1. **The logo is placed, never typed.** It is a drawn file in every language and every application.
   Setting `o / ra` in a typeface is not the logo, whatever it looks like at a glance.
2. **Scale is the only transformation.** Never rotate it, never stretch it, never outline it, never
   add a shadow, glow or effect to it, never rebuild it from letterforms.
3. **One variation per surface.** Two logos on one artifact means neither is the logo.
4. **The Clock is used whole.** Never cropped, never recomposed, never given content inside it.
5. **Nothing is for sale inside the Frame.** It holds principles and beliefs. The moment a price, an
   offer or a schedule enters it, the Frame stops meaning anything.
6. **The Word never appears bare.** No typed pairing, no word: use the Signature instead. The
   pairing is what the Word exists for.
7. **The Signature takes one companion, or none.** Never two on one surface: the Signature speaks
   once.

## 4 · Motion

The hour is written as data, in PT Mono: `1 / 00`. One hour, zero minutes, sixty untouched, the
moment before the class begins.

It counts down, and at zero the numerals settle into `o / ra`. The zero and the `o` share one form,
so when the hour runs out the zero does not empty, it becomes the face of the clock.

A clock, not a stopwatch. Slow, one movement, no flicker, no urgency, because a ticking countdown is
the language of interval apps and that is the register this brand refuses. It opens in PT Mono and
resolves into Helvetica, because `1 / 00` is the hour and `o / ra` is the clock that held it.

**Used as the end-frame, the app splash and the site landing. Nowhere else.**

# 02 · Colour

> **Red is heat, and heat is the discipline.**

ORA Red is never a ground. It is the heat itself: what motion leaves behind on whatever it crosses.
Every discipline has its own ground, and red arrives on top of it. Red appears in all five. What
changes is the dose, and the dose is the movement.

## 1 · Two tiers

The palette runs in two tiers with no overlap. Three colours belong to the three sides. Five belong
to the five disciplines. Eight colours, eight jobs, none doing double duty.

| Side | Colour | |
|---|---|---|
| **The Room** | Concrete | `#A5A5A5` |
| **The Movement** | ORA Red | `#D40000` |
| **The Woman**, the Brand, the Voice | Blood Red | `#660000` |

**The Movement's red moves. The Woman's red is where it has stopped.** ORA Red is heat in motion.
Blood Red is heat gone still: the carpet, the podcast, a woman sitting and speaking while everything
else in the brand is moving.

**The gradient.** `#D40000 → #660000` is the Movement handing off to the Woman. What she does,
falling into what she says. It is the one gradient in the system, used as a ground only, never
behind or on type.

## 2 · The five disciplines

Each discipline has one ground. Red crosses all five, dosed by how much the body is moving.

| Discipline | Ground | | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | Near-Black | `#0D0C0C` | A single edge. Force is greatest here and motion least, so red barely shows |
| **Running** | Charcoal | `#2E2C2B` | A rim light tracing the stride, one echo behind it |
| **Cycling** | Oxblood | `#2D0000` | Red fills the room as light. She is a silhouette inside it |
| **Pilates** | Steel | `#C6C6C6` | Two or three echoes offset along the movement, opacity stepping down |
| **Yoga** | White | `#FFFFFF` | One trace in the light, almost spent |

**Cycling is the hinge.** Two dark grounds lead into it, two light grounds lead out, and it is the
only ground with red already in it. Either side of that centre the dose thins by the same amount in
both directions: strength has not reached full heat, yoga is on the other side of it. Neither falls
to zero. The hour is never entirely cold.

## 3 · The palette

| | Value | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| **Blood Red** | `#660000` | The Brand, the Voice, the Woman |
| **ORA Red** | `#D40000` | The Movement. The heat across all five |
| **Oxblood** | `#2D0000` | Cycling |
| **Near-Black** | `#0D0C0C` | Strength |
| **Charcoal** | `#2E2C2B` | Running |
| **Concrete** | `#A5A5A5` | The Room |
| **Steel** | `#C6C6C6` | Pilates |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | Yoga |

## 4 · The guardrail

> **The atmosphere is behind and around. Never on the words.**

Type is always sharp, never blurred, glowing, trailed or grained. The mark is always sharp, and the
hand never blurs: it is the one fixed thing in a system built on motion. Blur, glow, echo, grain and
the gradient apply to the body or the ground, and to nothing else.

Without this, fully atmospheric becomes unreadable within a year, and stops looking premium the
first time someone outside the studio makes a post.

# 03 · Type

> **Helvetica Neue is the voice. PT Mono is the data.**

Every surface in the system is some ratio of the two, and the ratio is the tone. A manifesto is
nearly all voice. A timetable is nearly all data.

## 1 · Two faces

| | Face | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **The voice** | Helvetica Neue | The brand speaking. Headlines, body copy, display lines on signage, the mark itself |
| **The data** | PT Mono | Time and number. The timetable, prices, credits, discipline names in a set, wayfinding, captions, coordinates. Anything that is information rather than speech |

### Why the data face is a monospace

In a monospace, every character gets the same allotment of space. The narrowest letter gets what the
widest gets. That is the hour drawn as type: a fixed container that does not stretch for what it
holds. A yoga hour and a cycling hour are the same sixty minutes. An `i` and an `m` are the same
cell wide.

Three consequences follow.

Monospace is the lettering of machines that record time: the typewriter, the timetable, the ticket,
the receipt, the timestamp, the log. Wherever time is written down rather than spoken about, it is
written in fixed-width type. ORA's data face stands in that tradition on purpose.

A monospace is also the only typeface that is itself a grid. Characters sit in identical cells the
way minutes sit in hours, so on a system built at 60 units the data face carries the grid down into
the text.

And it holds one weight. No minute is bolder than another, and no fact in a timetable outranks the
fact beside it. The single weight is why the face was chosen. Data has sequence, and nothing else.

### Why PT Mono

Час is the brand's deepest word, so the data face has to be native in Cyrillic. PT Mono's Macedonian
glyphs (`Ѓ ѓ Ќ ќ Љ љ Њ њ Џ џ Ѕ ѕ Ј ј`) are drawn by ParaType, a Cyrillic foundry.

It is OFL licensed, so it travels free across the app, the site, print, the signage vendor and any
collaborator. And it ships in one usable cut, which enforces the one-weight rule by making the
alternative impossible to reach for.

*Note on the lineage: PT Mono is a 2011 digital face, not a typewriter face. The claim above is to
the tradition of monospace, not to this font's own history.*

## 2 · The meter

**ORA means the hour, so the system is set in minutes.** Every number in the geometry is a division
of sixty. Nothing else is admitted.

The grid is a **field of squares**. The square is **60, the hour**, and it halves to **30, the half
hour**, when a surface needs a finer field. **The margin is one square**, whichever field is in use.
Everything else lands on the square or its half.

On a 1080 × 1350 post that is 18 squares across at 60, or 36 at 30. The half field also closes the
format exactly: 1350 divides into 45 squares of 30, where the full field leaves a half square at the
foot.

On screen a unit is a pixel. In print, a point. The numbers never change, only the medium reading
them.

### The voice

| | Size | Line | On the grid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display** | 40 | 60 | One cell. A display line is one hour tall |
| **Half** | 30 | 45 | Three quarters |
| **Headline** | 20 | 30 | Half cell |
| **Body** | 10 | 15 | One quarter |
| **Micro** | 5 | 7.5 | Half quarter |

Line height is 1.5× everywhere, which lands every baseline on the grid. 30 is the half-cell step,
available where a surface needs something between display and headline.

### The data

Five sizes: **20 / 15 / 10 / 5 / 2.5.**

Data never displays. The largest data size is 20, half the display size. The hour is stated, never
shouted.

Data whispers one step lower than the voice can. The voice floor is 5. Data alone may drop to 2.5
for timestamps, coordinates and the finest line on a poster. At final output nothing that must be
read sits below 5, so 2.5 belongs to large formats printing at 2× or more.

The 15 is the pairing size. Mono runs wider than the voice at equal size, so when data sits beside a
20 headline it sets at 15. That three-to-four correction is why 15 exists: it appears next to the
voice, never as a hierarchy step of its own.

## 3 · Type carries the heat

The voice warms and cools with the discipline. The meter never changes, because the clock is the
same in every room.

**Weight is the load, and tracking follows weight.** The discipline order is already the load order,
so weight descends straight down it. Tracking is the optical correction that keeps each weight
legible: heavy needs tight, light needs air. One ramp, five steps, both moving together.

| Discipline | Weight | Tracking |
|---|---|---|
| **strength** | Black | `−2%` |
| **running** | Bold | `−1%` |
| **cycling** | Medium | `0` |
| **pilates** | Regular | `+4%` |
| **yoga** | Light | `+12%` |

Strength carries the most, so it is set in Black at minus two percent: the heaviest weight needs the
tightest tracking to hold together, which is also what the hour looks like, maximum load and minimum
air. Yoga is Light at plus twelve percent, the lightest weight and the widest tracking, and without
that air the line would collapse into grey.

**The heat is not in the type.** Red carries that, peaking at cycling and thinning either side of
it. Type carries the load, and it only ever moves in one direction.

### Which face carries a discipline

A discipline name sets in either face depending on the job it is doing. One question decides it: is
it a member of the set, or the subject of the surface?

| | Face | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| **A member of the set** | PT Mono | Lowercase, slash-joined, one weight, no tracking change. The timetable, the credit list, the discipline stack beside the mark, the Word pairing |
| **The subject of the surface** | Helvetica Neue | Carrying its own weight and tracking from the heat scale. A poster for one hour, a plate naming the room, a headline |

The rule is not arbitrary, and the reason is physical. **The heat scale can only exist in
Helvetica.** PT Mono holds one weight, so a discipline set in mono cannot carry Bold, Black or
Light. Mono is where the five are equal; Helvetica is where one gets to be hot or cold.

Descriptive text about a discipline is always voice, always Helvetica body. Wayfinding stays data,
because a plate telling her where she is does the same job as a timetable telling her when.

## 4 · The hand and the slash

The mark's diagonal and a typed slash are two different objects that happen to look alike.

| | The hand | The slash |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | The diagonal in the mark, and every drawn diagonal | Punctuation, typed in running text |
| **Angle** | **50° from horizontal, 40° from vertical** | Whatever the typeface draws, near 15° |
| **Rule** | Never redrawn, rotated, doubled or reversed | Never rotated to 50°, never redrawn as artwork |

A typed slash set at the hand's angle looks broken. A hand set at the typeface's angle stops being a
hand. Keeping them apart is what keeps both correct.

**The hand's angle: 50° from horizontal.** Stroke weight `9.5` on a `392` artboard, roughly 1:41,
which is how it holds its weight at any size. A designer needs one number and this is it.

### What the slash governs in text

| Application | Form |
|---|---|
| Timetable | `07 / 08 / 09 / 16 / 17 / 18 / 19` |
| Disciplines | `strength / running / cycling / pilates / yoga` |
| The three sides | `room / movement / woman` |
| Credits | `8 / 12 / 16 / 20` |
| Wayfinding | `atrium / movement room` |
| The hour as data | `1 / 00` |

It joins equals that could trade places. It never means *or*, because ORA's promise is that she does
not have to choose.

The semicolon does the other job, stringing qualities in sequence and taking a full stop, because it
is a sentence compressed into data: `space; material; light; hospitality.`

## 5 · Rules

1. **If it counts, it is mono.** Any number the brand states, whether a time, a price, a credit, a
   date or a distance, is data and sets in PT Mono.
2. **Data never tracks tight.** Negative tracking is urgency, and the clock refuses urgency. Mono
   may track open, and signage micro at `+14%` is a slower tick with the cells still equal. It never
   compresses.
3. **Data never bolds, italicises or shouts.** Uppercase is allowed as a register for labels and
   signage, never as emphasis inside data.
4. **Mono never carries a sentence.** The clock does not narrate. The moment mono takes a clause
   with a verb in it, the surface has two speakers.
5. **The voice sets at 40, 30, 20, 10 or 5, and nowhere between.** If a layout needs a size outside
   these, the layout is wrong.

# 04 · Art direction

> **The picture shows what the hour is about.**

Each discipline is about something different, so the camera points at a different thing. Sometimes
that thing is the woman, and often it is not. The ground and the dose of red are the ones already
set in Colour: one ground per hour, red crossing all five.

## 1 · The five hours

| Discipline | Ground | Red | The picture is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | Near-Black `#0D0C0C` | None | The body |
| **Running** | Charcoal `#2E2C2B` | A trace | The motion |
| **Cycling** | Oxblood `#2D0000` | Fills the room | The room |
| **Pilates** | Steel `#C6C6C6` | An echo | The detail |
| **Yoga** | White `#FFFFFF` | A trace | The light |

**Strength** is about effort, so the picture is the body. Low light, form readable, faces dark.
Nobody looks at the camera and nobody performs. The coldest ground in the set, and the only hour
with no red at all.

**Running** is about rhythm, so the picture is the movement rather than a held position. Figures
layered mid-stride, blurred at the edges. Red arrives as light on the move, marking where the body
has been.

**Cycling** is about the group and the dark, so the picture is the room and she is a shape inside
it. Bodies reduced to silhouette, no individual face. Red stops being a trace here and becomes the
whole field.

**Pilates** is about precision, so the picture is sharp and close. One woman, fully visible, evenly
lit. Precision cannot be photographed from across a room. Red sits behind her as an echo of the
movement she just made.

**Yoga** is about stillness, so the picture is the light and she dissolves into it. A single figure
on an empty field, the frame mostly nothing. The heat has gone and only a trace of red is left.

Reference artwork: `Movement/ORA_Movement_Strength.png`, `ORA_Movement_Running.png`,
`ORA_Movement_Indoor_Cycling.jpg`, `ORA_Movement_Pilates.jpg`, `ORA_Movement_Yoga.jpg`.

## 2 · Rules

1. **Point the camera at what the hour is about.** The body for strength, the motion for running,
   the room for cycling, the detail for pilates, the light for yoga. The woman is the subject in
   exactly one of the five.
2. **One ground per hour, and red on top of it.** The grounds are set in Colour and never swapped
   between disciplines.
3. **The type stays sharp.** Blur, echo, grain and trail apply to the body or the ground. The mark
   and the data never take an effect.
4. **Never retouched skin, never a body positioned to be looked at, never an expression she would
   not make.** Effort is allowed to show. Performance is not.

## Open

- **Helvetica Neue Cyrillic is unlicensed.** Needed for signage, app embedding, webfont and print.
  The signage vendor almost certainly needs a seat of their own. This blocks anything Cyrillic going
  to a fabricator.
- **`#A5A5A5` against the real floor.** Check the palette concrete against the studio's sealed floor
  in daylight. A grey that fights the real room shows in every photograph taken in it.
- **The cycling artwork runs brighter than the spec.** `ORA_Movement_Indoor_Cycling.jpg` is a full
  ORA Red field where Colour specifies Oxblood. The spec is the source; the artwork is an
  interpretation of it that sits warmer than written.
